    Deployment ran ahead of the evidence, and the evidence ran ahead of the rules.

    Carry an authorised change-control plan

    Predetermined Change Control Plans are the mechanism for legal model updates. Roughly 26 devices had one as of May 2025 — the rest must re-submit to retrain.

    The gap that governance has to close

    The authorisation record describes image-analysis software cleared once and frozen. The tools spreading fastest through hospitals are something else: ambient scribes, inbox drafters, triage chatbots, coding assistants and prior-authorisation engines — general-purpose models applied to clinical work, often outside the medical-device definition altogether, and updated by a vendor without notice.

    That is the live governance problem. Not whether AI belongs in medicine, but who is accountable when a model that was never reviewed as a device quietly changes the standard of care.

    • 01
      Device or not a device

      Intended purpose decides. The same model can be a Class II device in one configuration and unregulated software in another.

    • 02
      Frozen or adaptive

      Traditional device law assumed a fixed artefact. Models retrain. Change-control plans are the bridge, and they remain rare.

    • 03
      Approved or actually working

      Market authorisation is a point-in-time judgement. Performance drifts with the population, the scanner and the workflow.
